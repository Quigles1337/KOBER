## 0. Headline finding for statement fidelity (E3)

**The paper does not state a uniqueness theorem over its constraint system.** Its own words
(p. 6): "Clearly a constraint-based approach cannot decipher the values of Linear A fraction
signs on its own, mainly because the system is severely underconstrained" — Constraints 1–6
admit **3,794,740** solutions (Simulation 2). The headline value assignment emerges only after a
four-measure goodness ranking (§5 below), and the paper presents it as a **conditional
hypothesis** whose H and A components are explicitly "tentative". Any Phase-1 theorem claiming
"the constraints force unique values" would be a strengthening the paper does not support (E3
violation). See §7 for the exact claim.

Also load-bearing: the constitution's shorthand "summation-line constraint system" (§P0.2 of
KOBER-PROGRAM.md) does not describe this paper. On totals the paper says (p. 2): "As no Linear A
inscription containing totals of numeral phrases is without reading or calculation problems
(Montecchi, 2009, 2013), it is difficult to deduce these mathematical values." The constraint
system is a set of six structural premises over attestation data — only one anchor text (HT 104)
is a summation, and it feeds Constraint 1, not a general summation apparatus.

## 2. (b) "Summation lines"

See §0: there is no general summation-line corpus in this paper. Arithmetic anchoring rests on
HT 104 (a sum with total), PE 1 (a proportion), and HT Zd 156 (a progression), all feeding
Constraint 1 only. The paper's totals-are-problematic statement (p. 2, quoted in §0) is itself a
citable, register-worthy fact about the corpus.

## 4. The six constraints (p. 3–4, §2.1, premises quoted/condensed)

1. "The values of J, E and F are 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8, respectively."
2. "Combinations of fractional signs are additive."
3. "The same fractional value cannot be represented by more than one sign, or by both a sign and
   a combination of signs." (Economy premise; the converse — several combinations sharing a value
   — is allowed. Implication stated: no sign for 3/4, since J E = 3/4.)
4. "Any fractional sign or sum of combined fractions (Table 1) in a numeral phrase is less than
   1." (Possible cumulative-notation exceptions are MM II and excluded from the sample.)
5. "Any fraction is larger than the fraction placed to the right or below it. Accordingly,
   unproblematic inscriptions show the following relationships: J > E > F > K > L2 > L4; J > A;
   J > H; E > B; L (?) > L2." (fn. 3 flags the two doubtful E J counterexamples.)
6. "The L series is divisive": series shape x, x/2, x/3, x/4, x/6 for L, L2, L3, L4, L6, with
   horizontal-line divisor value hl = 1 chosen over hl = 10 (hl = 10 would make the absence of a
   single-stroke L/10 sign inexplicable; multiplicative/additive readings violate Constraint 5;
   subtractive goes negative).

Solver stack (p. 4): "We expressed the problem by means of the MiniZinc constraint programming
language (Nethercote et al., 2007) and the Gecode solver (Gecode Team, 2006)."

  range of fractions at 1/2 … 1/320. We also included three typologically attested values with
  higher numerators, 5/6, 3/4, and 2/3." After removing 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 (Constraint 1) and 3/4
  (= J E): "This range comprises 318 possible values." (Executor check: 319 unit fractions

- Restricted candidate set for the non-L signs (A, B, D, H, K), verbatim including its printed
  order quirk "1/108, 1/100" (p. 5): "Thus, our set comprises: 5/6, 2/3, 1/3, 1/5, 1/6, 3/20,
  1/7, 1/9, 1/10, 1/12, 1/15, 1/16, 1/20, 1/24, 1/30, 1/32, 1/36, 1/40, 1/48, 1/50, 1/60, 1/64,
  1/70, 1/72, 1/80, 1/84, 1/90, 1/96, 1/108, 1/100, 1/120, 1/160, 1/200, 1/240, 1/300, 1/320."
  (36 values; built from: all typological values incl. Eye-of-Horus base-2 and Grantha base-20;
  all powers of 10; products of 10 with sub-bases 2 and 5; multiples of 10 up to 100; divisors of
  240; multiples of 12 up to 120; plus 1/7 and 1/9 for the sign-D oddity.)
- L-series domain (p. 5): "we defined all values between 1/2 and 1/320 as possible for the L

- Derived bounds stated (p. 5): L2 ≥ 3/320 (≈1/107) "because L2 = L6*3 (cf. Constraint 6) and

## 6. Simulations, tests, and goodness measures

- **Simulation 1** (L included as 13th variable): "2,172,836 solutions" (p. 5); lowest (best)
  G_FreqVSDiv = 0.14284 (p. 6).
- **Sign-L tests (§3, p. 5–6):** (i) common-divisor rank of L is "always very anomalous" across
  solutions (Table 4); (ii) χ² goodness-of-fit with P(L) = 2/3, P(L2) = 1/3 within the L-series:
  observing 1 L against 26 L2 has p = 4×10⁻¹² — L's rarity is incompatible with a divisive
  L-series head; L6 (P = 1/4, 2 observed) is not anomalous (Table 5). Conclusion: "we therefore
  propose that the sign shape documented as 'LL2' is anomalous and thus exclude L from our
  analysis" (p. 6).
- **Simulation 2** (12 variables, L excluded; doubtful MA 4 / CHIC #068 attestations not used):
  "3,794,740 solutions", of which 394,905 beat Simulation 1's best G_FreqVSDiv (best ≈ 0.00881)
  (p. 6).
- **Measures (p. 6–7):**
  - *G_FreqVSDiv* = 1 − SRCC(attestation-frequency rank, common-divisor-count rank).
  - *G_AmbCombs* = number of pairs of **attested** combinations (Table 1) with equal value.
  - *G_Typ* = number of sign assignments not typologically documented (every Simulation-2
    solution has ≥ 2, p. 6).
  - *MULT* = product of the three (fn. 6: multiplication ≈ logical conjunction); top 5000
    lowest-MULT solutions retained.
  - *G_Optimal* (applied to the 5000; p. 6–7, Tables 6–7): incompleteness + ambiguity over the
    range 1/2 … (D−1)/D, D = denominator of the solution's smallest value; combinations of at
    most 4 signs (fn. 5: DDDD is the longest attested combination); per-denominator weighting
    1/(D−1) (missing/ambiguous low-denominator values penalized more); each component averaged
    over the number of denominators, then summed. **Ambiguity flag A-3:** whether the
    denominator grid runs over every integer 2…D or only denominators present in the solution is
    schematic in Tables 6–7 (rows 2,3,4,5); replication must disambiguate.

## 7. (e) The exact claim as stated

Verbatim, p. 8, §5 (the paper's own summary of its result):

> "Our results can be expressed by a simple hypothesis: given Constraints 1–6, *if* the Linear A
> fractions had values that are typologically attested or part of a typologically attested base
> or sub-base of numerical notations; and *if* Linear A fractions formed an optimal system, in
> which the documented combinations of signs represented the maximum number of values within its
> own fractional base, while at the same time generating a minimum number of redundant
> additions; *then* the correct values of Linear A signs comprise J = 1/2, E = 1/4, F = 1/8,
> B = 1/5, D = 1/6, K = 1/10, L2 = 1/20, L3 = 1/30, L4 = 1/40, L6 = 1/60 (Tables 8 and 9). The
> assignments H = 1/16 and A = 1/24 are tentative because they do not violate typology, but
> their optimality is not as consistent."

Supporting result cascade (p. 8, §4.2–4.4):
- Two solutions tie for best G_Optimal (0.67094), agreeing on B = 1/5, D = 1/6, K = 1/10,
  L2 = 1/20, L3 = 1/30, L4 = 1/40, L6 = 1/60 and differing only by H/A ↔ 1/32/1/36.
- K = 1/10 with the L-series values appears in the 48 best-G_Optimal solutions
  (0.67094–0.75379) and 52 others: 100 of the 5000.
- Of those 100, 86 contain B = 1/5, D = 1/6 — "the arrangement in the 28 solutions with the best
  G_Optimal metrics (0.67094–0.72676)". Stated alternatives: "B = 1/6, D = 1/5 (12 solutions,
  G_Optimal ≤ 0.71724), B = 1/5, D = 1/12 (1 solution, 0.79232) and B = 1/9, D = 1/6 (1
  solution, 0.80510)".
- H/A among the 28 best: H ∈ {1/16, 1/24, 1/32, 1/36, 1/48, 1/64, 1/72, 1/84},
  A ∈ {1/24, 1/32, 1/36, 1/48, 1/64, 1/72, 1/84}; "specific values for H and A cannot be
  postulated through optimality" (p. 8); H = 1/16 is "the only value possible for H … that is
  not also possible for A"; H = 1/16 argued from palaeography (crooked-shape series with J, E,
  F → binary 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16), A = 1/24 via A A = 2/24 = 1/12 = 5/60 and divisors-of-240
  structure — the paper concedes "the arrangement H = 1/16, A = 1/24 has a worse G_Optimal
  metric than H = 1/16 and A = 1/36 or 1/64 or 1/72" (p. 8).

**Logical form (executor analysis, feeds P0.4):** the claim is a *conditional characterization
relative to a defined objective*, not uniqueness from constraints. Candidate faithful formal
statements for Phase 1, in ascending strength:
1. (Enumeration) Over the exact domains of §5, the solution set of Constraints 1–6 has
   cardinality 3,794,740 — pending disambiguation of flags A-1/A-2/A-3 by replication.
2. (Anchored subset) Every Constraints-1–6 solution satisfies the stated order/bound corollaries
   (e.g. L2 ≥ 3/320; L-series < 1/9) — provable from the constraint definitions alone.
3. (Optimum characterization) Relative to the four measures *as defined*, the minimizers over
   the filtered space carry exactly the ten firm assignments {J, E, F, B, D, K, L2, L3, L4, L6}
   = {1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/5, 1/6, 1/10, 1/20, 1/30, 1/40, 1/60}, with H/A underdetermined as the
   paper states.
   A theorem of form (3) is honest; a theorem claiming uniqueness from Constraints 1–6 alone
   would be false by the paper's own count.

## 8. Internal observations in the published text (recorded, not adjudicated)

Verified against page renders; these are replication targets, not accusations. All are
consistent with an honest paper; the engine (Phase 2) adjudicates.

- **OBS-1.** Table 4 (p. 5) prints total attestations J = 138; Tables 2 (p. 2) and 8 (p. 9)
  print J = 137.
- **OBS-2.** "G_Optimal ≤ 0.71724" for the 12 B = 1/6, D = 1/5 solutions (p. 8) sits oddly
  against "the 28 solutions with the best G_Optimal metrics (0.67094–0.72676)" all having
  B = 1/5, D = 1/6 — if 12 alternatives were ≤ 0.71724 they would intrude into that band. A ≤/≥
  slip is plausible; only replication can resolve which figure is right.
- **OBS-3.** "B and K are higher than L2 (Constraint 5)" (p. 5): K > L2 follows from the printed
  Constraint-5 chain, but **B > L2 does not appear in the printed relation list** (only E > B).
  Either an unstated relation was used or the citation is loose — model reconstruction must
  decide, and R0 must record which inscriptions would ground B > L2 if it is real.
- **OBS-4.** "This means that L3 must equal L2*1.5" (p. 5) inverts the series arithmetic (x/2 =
  1.5 × x/3, so L2 = 1.5 × L3); the surrounding argument (consecutive ratio 3:2 unattested
  typologically among low values) is unaffected.
- **OBS-5.** Table 6's worked example (p. 7) totals (0 + 1/2 + 0 + 1/4)/4 = 3/16 and prints
  "= 18.6 %"; 3/16 = 18.75%.
- **OBS-6.** Appendix B and C headers (p. 11–12) print "K = 10" for K = 1/10.
